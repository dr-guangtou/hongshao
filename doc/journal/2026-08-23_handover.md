
## Problems / Blockers

### The two remaining defects, stated without jargon

**(a) The inner deficit — a MISPLACED-mass problem, not a missing-mass one.**
The quantity is, per galaxy, `log10[M*_model(<R)] − log10[M*_truth(<R)]`,
median over galaxies. Negative = the model predicts LESS than the simulation.

| R | median log10(model/truth) | in words |
|---|---|---|
| 3 kpc | **−0.10** | model 21% too light |
| ~10 kpc | ~0 | correct |
| 20–70 kpc | +0.02 | model ~5% too heavy |
| 148 kpc | ~0 | correct |

The total is nearly right (amplitude bias −0.008 dex at z=0.4), so **the model
builds the right amount of stellar mass and puts too much of it at 20–70 kpc
instead of in the centre.** This is the standing compact-central defect
(exp47/48/53) at −21% instead of −39%..−47%. **No profile family fixes it** —
gompertz_log, moffat and sersic give the same curve.

**(b) The z=2 amplitude deficit, and what "halo-mass tilt" means.**
The median of `log10[M*_model(<100 kpc)] − log10[M*_truth(<100 kpc)]` is
**−0.026 dex at z=2** (the typical galaxy is 5.8% too light) against −0.008 to
+0.010 at z <= 1.5.

**HALO-MASS TILT, defined**: for a fixed radius and epoch, take each galaxy's
residual `y = log10[M*_model(<R)] − log10[M*_truth(<R)]`, fit a straight line of
`y` against `log10(halo mass)` across the population; **the tilt is that slope,
in dex of stellar-mass error per dex of halo mass.** Tilt = 0 means the model is
equally right for light and heavy haloes. At z=2 it is **−0.061 to −0.122
dex/dex at EVERY radius** — radius-independent, so an amplitude failure, not a
shape failure. Over this sample's 2.4 dex halo-mass range that is a **0.29 dex
swing**: accurate for the lightest haloes, increasingly too light for the
heaviest.

## The user's three questions (answer these first next session)

1. **Does "inner deficit" mean the model predicts lower stellar mass than the
   simulation?** — Yes. See (a) above; answered in-session, recorded here.
2. **"Median bias −0.026 dex" — bias of what? What is a "halo-mass tilt"?** —
   Both defined in (b) above. **Standing instruction from the user: never
   create a term without defining it in place.**
3. **The delayed kernel — is fitting it empirically defensible?** The user's
   point: stellar growth genuinely lags halo assembly, but that lag is governed
   by real physics, so a free fit may not be legitimate.
   **The agreed refinement**: a single free `tau` would conflate at least three
   distinct processes — gas infall/cooling (halo dynamical time), the
   star-formation/depletion timescale (ISM physics), and merger + orbital decay
   for accreted stars (dynamical friction, Gyr-long for minor mergers, and
   dependent on mass ratios that **DiffMAH has smoothed away**). The proposal is
   therefore to anchor it:

   ```
   tau(t_j) = eta * t_dyn(t_j),   t_dyn = R200c(t_j) / V200c(t_j)
   ```

   one dimensionless `eta`, computable from quantities already held at all 73
   snapshots. **`eta` is then interpretable and falsifiable: if stars follow
   halo assembly on a dynamical timescale, `eta` should be O(1); if the fit
   wants `eta` >> 1 or << 1, the mechanism is not dynamical and we say so
   rather than keep the parameter.** THIS IS NOT YET AGREED — it is the
   proposal to discuss.
