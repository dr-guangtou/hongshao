# exp62 — Multi-epoch analytic CoG fitting atlas

Status: COMPLETE. Independent-epoch atlas retained; no joint smooth-in-time
analytic family adopted. Epoch-local halo associations recorded as descriptive
results, not a portable model or established physics.

## Question

How well can a finite, scientifically distinct set of one- and two-component
analytic families describe every usable curve of growth (CoG) from redshift
0.4 to 2? Which fitted parameters are numerically identifiable, which evolve
smoothly, and which aspects of the epoch-local mass-accretion history and
concurrent halo concentration are associated with which radial aspects of the
stellar-mass distribution?

This experiment is a descriptive foundation. It does not attempt to graduate a
portable emulator, and it does not assume an inner--early-assembly or
outer--recent-assembly connection. Those are hypotheses to test with controls.

## Data and samples

- Primary observable: the 24-point CoG-derived stellar mass profile from 2.0
  to 148.2 kpc at redshifts 0.4, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0.
- Representation sample: every galaxy with a finite, positive, monotonic CoG
  at the epoch being fitted. Epochs may therefore have different sample sizes.
- Evolution sample: the established 2,397 matched progenitor tracks with valid
  CoGs at all five epochs.
- Quality-clean halo sample: the established 2,395 tracks after removing the
  two broken progenitor matches.
- Epoch-local halo sample: the established 2,365-track overlap with valid
  concentration histories and truncated-history DiffMAH fits.

The driver writes an attrition table with the actual counts and exclusion
reasons. The reproduced population contains 3,380 finite, positive, monotonic
CoGs at every one of the five epochs, 3,380 valid five-epoch tracks, 2,395
quality-clean halo-matched tracks, and 2,365 tracks with both epoch-local
DiffMAH and concurrent concentration.
Masses are h-free `log10(M / Msun)`. Official DiffMAH masses in Msun/h receive
the established `+log10(1/h)` correction before use.

## Candidate taxonomy

Every family is normalized by its mass within the measured outer aperture,
`M_148 = M*(<148.2 kpc)`. Component fractions are also defined within that
finite aperture so extrapolated mass is not silently treated as observed.

### One-component families

1. Projected Sersic: free `M_148`, `R50`, and index `n`.
2. Moffat: free `M_148`, `R50`, and outer index `gamma > 1`.
3. Gompertz in log radius: a power-law outer tail and rapidly suppressed core.
4. Gompertz in linear radius: an exponential-like outer-cutoff control.
5. Log-logistic: a symmetric sigmoid in log radius with power-law core and
   tail.
6. Richards generalized logistic: a free asymmetry that nests log-Gompertz
   and log-logistic limits.
7. Radial-slope sigmoid with asymptotic outer CoG slope fixed to zero.
8. Radial-slope sigmoid with a free asymptotic outer CoG slope.

### Two-component families

9. Fixed-index double Sersic. Use the predeclared global index pairs
   `(n_compact, n_extended) = (1,1), (2,1), (4,1), (4,2)`, ordered component
   radii, and select the pair on training galaxies only at each epoch.
10. Unrestricted double Sersic. Fit two free indices with ordered radii and
    treat it as an explicit degeneracy stress test, not an expected winner.
11. Compact Sersic plus extended Moffat. Use the predeclared global grid
    `n = (0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25, 2.0)` and
    `gamma = (1.1, 1.25, 1.4, 1.7)`, with training-only selection at each
    epoch. Do not infer per-galaxy global indices in this constrained version.

Flexible reconstruction ceilings are PCA with three and five shape modes and
the Exp51 five-readable-coordinate decoder. They are references, not analytic
families and are not eligible for physical parameter interpretation.

## Stage 0 — mechanics and sub-minute gate

Before fitting real galaxies:

- assert positivity, monotonicity, finite-aperture normalization, parameter
  transforms, analytic limits, and the known nesting of Richards;
- recover synthetic profiles generated by every family from deliberately
  displaced starts;
- verify component ordering removes the exact label-exchange symmetry;
- verify every reported metric on an exact reconstruction;
- run the complete Stage 1 path on 90 galaxies stratified by stellar mass and
  require measured wall time below 60 seconds before a full calculation.

If the 90-galaxy path exceeds one minute, improve or narrow the implementation
before running the complete population. Never extrapolate the timing.

## Stage 1 — common independent-epoch family screen

Fit every candidate independently to every valid galaxy at every epoch using
bounded deterministic multi-start optimization. The common initial fitting
objective is uniform-radius RMS in log10 cumulative mass. All models are then
judged with the same metrics, regardless of their number of parameters:

- CoG RMS over 2--148, 5--148, and 5--30 kpc;
- maximum absolute fractional CoG error over all radii and over `R >= 5 kpc`;
- log shell-mass and differenced-density RMS;
- errors in `M_148`, `R20`, `R50`, `R80`, and `R90`;
- optimizer failure, boundary incidence, agreement among near-optimal starts,
  and the smallest-to-largest scaled Jacobian singular-value ratio.

Complexity is shown as an accuracy--stability Pareto comparison. Raw-CoG
AIC/BIC is not used because the 24 cumulative radii repeat strongly correlated
enclosed-mass information. Candidate global shape grids are selected within
galaxy folds and evaluated only on the held-out galaxies.

Required figures are the family-by-epoch metric atlas, direct measured/model
CoGs with residuals for best/typical/worst and mass-stratified galaxies, and a
complexity--accuracy--conditioning comparison. Every figure is saved as PNG
and PDF using `hongshao.plotting`.

## Stage 2 — objective sensitivity and identifiability

Advance three roles rather than forcing one universal winner:

1. the most accurate stable one-component family;
2. the most accurate stable constrained two-component family;
3. unrestricted double Sersic as the flexible accuracy/degeneracy ceiling.

Refit them under the focused objective matrix

```
quantity = CoG | shell mass | density
residual = absolute linear difference / M_148 | log10 difference
```

with RMS radial aggregation and uniform radius weights. Fractional residuals
and per-dex radial weights are secondary sensitivity tests. These are fitting
objectives, not likelihoods: the stored CoGs do not provide a validated full
radial covariance. Density differencing omits the central aperture exactly;
shell mass is retained because it is bijective with the CoG and preserves it.

For each advanced family, test widely separated starts, a second optimizer on
a mass/compactness/epoch-stratified subset, exact synthetic recovery,
alternating-radius jackknifes, fits with `R >= 5 kpc`, profiled parameter scans
with every other parameter reoptimized, scaled-Jacobian singular values,
boundary incidence, and theoretical inner/outer asymptotes. For mixtures,
measure component merging, vanishing fractions, and raw-parameter movement at
nearly fixed decoded CoG. A family may remain a useful representation while
its unstable raw parameters are explicitly barred from physical interpretation.

## Stage 3 — scaling relations and redshift evolution

For every surviving family, store both its raw fitted parameters and common
decoded observables:

- `M_148` and the 2-kpc mass fraction;
- `R20`, `R50`, `R80`, and `R90`;
- stellar mass in 2--10, 10--30, 30--50, 50--100, and 100--148 kpc;
- logarithmic CoG and density slopes in predeclared inner, intermediate, and
  outer radial ranges.

At every epoch, measure median trends and scatter versus total stellar mass,
current halo mass, and concurrent concentration. On matched tracks, test
constant, linear, and quadratic evolution in cosmic time. Judge smoothness by
leaving out each interior epoch, decoding the interpolated parameters, and
comparing its CoG error with both the direct fit and nearest-epoch copying.
Only families with smooth independent parameters proceed to a joint
smooth-in-time CoG fit. Report cases where raw parameters jump along a
degeneracy while common decoded observables remain smooth.

## Stage 4 — interpreting the epoch-local halo--CoG relation

Use DiffMAH fitted only to information available through the profile epoch and
`c_200c` measured at that epoch. Descendant-conditioned features are a
secondary track-history diagnostic, not the primary interpretation.

For every identifiable raw or decoded target:

- remove current-halo-mass trends inside each of five galaxy folds;
- fit simple standardized linear/additive associations with the four
  epoch-local DiffMAH parameters and concurrent concentration;
- measure marginal mass-conditioned and conditional complete-model responses;
- bootstrap galaxies and inspect predictor covariance and coefficient
  conditioning;
- repeat current-mass-only, mass-conditioned shuffled-DiffMAH, and shuffled-
  concentration controls;
- decode supported parameter responses into radial changes in CoG, shell mass,
  and density.

Five-fold cross-fitting is used to prevent an apparent association from being
created by flexible regression, not to claim a production emulator. All
results are associations. An unstable coefficient, a disagreement between
marginal and conditional signs, or parity with a shuffle is recorded as such.

## Visual QA and decision

Every promising candidate receives the standard QA battery: aperture, annular,
and outskirt masses; CoGs and residuals in halo- and stellar-mass bins;
density; observational and size planes; residual distributions; representative
individual objects; and cross-epoch stellar-growth diagnostics. Average CoGs
in halo-mass bins and stellar-mass planes are mandatory visual gates.

### Final visual record (predeclared before the synthesis driver)

The completed numerical record will be accompanied by a deliberately compact
visual synthesis. It will not refit any profile or change any selection:

1. compare log-radius Gompertz, constrained Sersic--Moffat, and unrestricted
   double Sersic using population statistics: average CoGs and residuals in
   current halo- and stellar-mass bins, stellar-mass planes, mass--size
   relations, and CDF residuals for aperture, annular, and outskirt masses;
2. show only the ten worst galaxies for each representation, ranked by its
   worst-epoch CoG RMS, to identify recurrent failure shapes rather than to
   produce galaxy-by-galaxy QA;
3. compare model performance across all five epochs using CoG, density,
   aperture/outskirt mass, and size errors;
4. compare the three representations against the measured CoGs in common,
   decoded scaling relations with total stellar mass at representative
   redshifts;
5. show the matched-track population evolution of the identifiable raw
   Gompertz and constrained Sersic--Moffat parameters, split by final stellar
   mass so that changing population mix is not mistaken for time evolution;
6. show mass-conditioned, binned scaling relations between every identifiable
   constrained Sersic--Moffat coordinate and every epoch-local DiffMAH plus
   concentration coordinate, then state the same relation symbolically beside
   its measured coefficient matrices. These are descriptive associations, not
   causal diagrams.

The population figures use the 2,395 quality-clean matched tracks so every
panel has a common sample. The complete synthesis path must first run on the
established 90-galaxy mass-stratified sample in under one minute. Every figure
is written as both PNG and PDF.

The experiment need not select one family. A valid endpoint is a documented
trade space in which the best profile compression and the best identifiable
parameterization differ. No experiment-local code graduates into `hongshao/`
without a separate decision after the atlas is complete.

## Results

### Validation and population

The family mechanics pass for all 33 concrete fitted variants. The complete
Stage 1 path over 90 unique, halo-mass-stratified galaxies passed in 15.39
seconds after one persistent worker pool replaced repeated pool startup; the
first two implementations took 73.82 and 64.80 seconds and were rejected
before any full run. The full atlas contains 16,900 galaxy--epoch CoGs from
3,380 galaxies and finished in 1,535.03 seconds.

The objective matrix passed its 90-profile gate in 21.32 seconds and the full
18-cell calculation finished in 2,586.43 seconds. The complete robustness path
over 90 profiles passed in 10.41 seconds after diagnostic refits were correctly
continued from the original solution; the corresponding 200-profile full
diagnostics finished in 12.92 seconds. Stage 3 passed in 8.66 seconds and used
2,395 tracks in the full calculation. Stage 4 passed in 7.80 seconds and used
2,365 tracks, 200 galaxy bootstraps, and 32 mass-conditioned shuffles per
control and epoch in the 26.26-second full calculation.

### Stage 1: independent-epoch representation trade space

The table reports the median of the five per-epoch medians. Every accuracy is
measured against the original TNG CoG or its differenced density; smaller is
better.

| representation | parameters | full-CoG RMS (dex) | density RMS (dex) | absolute R90 error (dex) | within 1% of a bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| log-radius Gompertz | 3 | 0.00765 | 0.12923 | 0.07788 | 2.72% |
| constrained Sersic + Moffat | 4 per galaxy + fold/epoch global shapes | 0.00439 | 0.09827 | 0.04263 | 3.64% |
| unrestricted double Sersic | 6 | 0.00263 | 0.08469 | 0.01664 | 14.44% |
| PCA(3) reference | 4 including amplitude | 0.00357 | 0.07560 | 0.01709 | -- |
| PCA(5) reference | 6 including amplitude | 0.00156 | 0.05271 | 0.00616 | -- |
| five readable coordinates | 5 | 0.00400 | 0.09392 | 0.02755 | -- |

The unrestricted double Sersic is the most accurate analytic compression, but
its boundary rate, weak scaled Jacobians, and profiled component-shape valleys
make its raw parameters unsuitable for physical interpretation. The constrained
Sersic--Moffat is the best compromise between CoG accuracy and parameter
conditioning. Log-radius Gompertz is the stable three-parameter single-family
reference.

### Stage 2: the fitting objective changes what is recovered

No one objective dominates CoG, density, and size fidelity. For the constrained
Sersic--Moffat, changing from log-CoG to absolute-CoG fitting worsens median
full-CoG RMS from 0.00439 to 0.00519 dex, but improves density RMS from 0.09827
to 0.09205 dex and absolute R90 error from 0.04263 to 0.03522 dex. For the
unrestricted double Sersic, the same change worsens full-CoG RMS from 0.00263
to 0.00349 dex while improving density RMS from 0.08469 to 0.06431 dex and
absolute R90 error from 0.01664 to 0.00744 dex. Log-shell fitting improves
local density and R90 still further, but sacrifices too much cumulative-CoG
accuracy. Density-only fitting is rejected: the central aperture is invisible
after differencing, allowing CoGs to move by 0.049--0.133 dex even when the
differenced density objective is small.

Exact synthetic recovery and the alternate optimizer reproduce the decoded
profiles to numerical precision. Alternating-radius refits change the median
decoded CoG by only 0.0010--0.0018 dex. Removing all radii below 5 kpc changes
it by 0.0160--0.0252 dex, demonstrating that the outer CoG cannot reconstruct
the omitted core. The unrestricted double Sersic moves 24--26% of an allowed
parameter range in this test while changing its decoded CoG less than the
simpler families; this is direct evidence for raw-parameter degeneracy.

### Stage 3: population scaling is smooth, individual tracks are not

Median decoded relations with total stellar mass and concurrent halo mass vary
regularly across redshift, and the common decoded observables are much more
consistent between analytic families than the raw mixture parameters. This
population-level smoothness does not close a held-epoch test for individual
galaxies.

Leaving out each of z=0.7, 1.0, and 1.5, fitting a quadratic in cosmic time to
the other four raw parameter vectors, and decoding the omitted epoch gives the
following median full-CoG RMS against the original TNG CoG:

| representation and objective | direct epoch fit (dex) | quadratic raw-parameter interpolation (dex) | quadratic analytic-CoG interpolation (dex) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gompertz, log CoG | 0.00757 | 0.05983 | 0.05380 |
| Gompertz, absolute CoG | 0.00979 | 0.06179 | 0.05490 |
| constrained Sersic--Moffat, log CoG | 0.00429 | 0.05979 | 0.05355 |
| constrained Sersic--Moffat, absolute CoG | 0.00505 | 0.06111 | 0.05378 |
| unrestricted double Sersic, log CoG | 0.00252 | 0.06103 | 0.05349 |
| unrestricted double Sersic, absolute CoG | 0.00328 | 0.06399 | 0.05380 |

Quadratically interpolating the measured 24-point CoG itself gives 0.05351 dex.
Its near equality with analytic-CoG interpolation shows that the analytic
representation adds negligible error to this test. Five-epoch sampling plus
real per-galaxy growth irregularity sets the floor; raw-parameter motion adds a
smaller extra penalty. No family therefore proceeds to a joint smooth-in-time
fit. Retain independent-epoch fits and model population coefficients across
redshift only when that is the scientific target.

### Stage 4: descriptive epoch-local halo--CoG associations

The five-fold relation first removes a quadratic current-halo-mass trend, then
uses standardized residuals of epoch-local `[logmp, logtc, early, late,
c_200c]`. The full calculation includes 133 targets: measured decoded
summaries, decoded summaries from all six advanced family/objective
combinations, and the raw parameters of the identifiable Gompertz and
constrained Sersic--Moffat fits. Unrestricted double-Sersic raw parameters are
excluded by the Stage 2 degeneracy evidence.

For the primary constrained Sersic--Moffat log-CoG representation, the complete
relation improves median full-CoG RMS against TNG from 0.11155 dex for current
halo mass alone to 0.08985 dex, and density RMS from 0.19792 to 0.17593 dex.
The mass-conditioned predictor condition number is only 1.79--1.98 across the
five epochs; the complete relation places 0.0085% of parameter vectors on a
bound.

At z=0.4, the real halo inputs reduce held-out RMS relative to current halo
mass alone by 6.85% for R20, 8.50% for R50, 7.76% for R80, 7.52% for R90,
22.03% for the 10--30 kpc stellar mass, 24.25% for 50--100 kpc, and 23.82% for
100--148 kpc. Shuffling all four DiffMAH coordinates within halo-mass bins
reduces the four size gains to -0.15--+0.39%, while retaining 7.53--8.38% gains
in the three radial mass blocks from real concentration. Shuffling
concentration retains 19.44--20.96% of the radial-block gains from real
DiffMAH. Thus both information sources contribute to the outskirts, while the
size/concentration signal in this linear description is carried by the MAH
coordinates.

The primary standardized conditional-response pattern correlates at 0.973
with the same analysis of decoded summaries measured directly from the TNG
CoGs. It correlates at 0.923--0.965 with the other analytic families and at
0.998 with the absolute-CoG constrained Sersic--Moffat fit. This representation
robustness supports the statistical pattern, but it does not turn a conditional
association into physics. In particular, this experiment does not establish an
inner--early-MAH connection or an outer--recent-assembly connection.

The standard QA figure shows that the cross-fitted mean reproduces average CoG
shapes in halo-mass bins but compresses the scatter in the inner--outer stellar
mass planes, as a deterministic conditional mean must. This is additional
evidence not to treat the Stage 4 relation as a generative population model.

### Final population-level visual record

The revised complete visual path passed on 90 halo-mass-stratified galaxies in
27.96 seconds, below the predeclared one-minute limit. The full figures use the
2,395 quality-clean matched tracks and finished in 41.07 seconds. No profiles
were refitted: every panel reads the completed Stage 1--4 artifacts.

The population figures confirm the numerical hierarchy while showing where it
comes from. The unrestricted double Sersic has the smallest median full-CoG RMS
against the measured TNG CoG, 0.00243 dex, compared with 0.00422 dex for the
constrained Sersic--Moffat and 0.00739 dex for log-Gompertz. Its median density
RMS is likewise best at 0.08304 dex, compared with 0.10125 and 0.13212 dex.
The mass-binned residual curves show that this is not merely a median-score
advantage: double Sersic usually has the smallest coherent shape residual,
while Gompertz has the strongest mass-dependent inner and outer curvature
errors.

The stellar-mass planes and mass--size relations are close for all three
families through 100 kpc. The discriminating population statistic is the
100--148 kpc mass distribution. Its CDF residuals show a coherent shift for
every family even when cumulative CoG RMS is small; the signs differ between
the unrestricted double Sersic and the two simpler families. Thus excellent
cumulative reconstruction does not guarantee recovery of the low-mass outer
shell distribution. This is the main reason to retain CDF and differenced-mass
QA beside CoG RMS.

The three worst-ten galleries expose different recurrent failures rather than
showing arbitrary individual galaxies. Gompertz failures are broad one-hump
curvature mismatches that can exceed 0.1 dex in the mass-pinned CoG. The
constrained Sersic--Moffat reduces that mismatch but retains two-hump residuals
when one compact and one extended component cannot follow intermediate radial
structure. The unrestricted double Sersic failures are smaller and more local,
but Stage 2 still bars its raw component parameters from interpretation.

The direct parameter--DiffMAH figure removes a quadratic concurrent-halo-mass
trend from both axes and standardizes each epoch before binning. The fitted
`log_mstar_148` coordinate has the clearest residual relations: positive with
epoch-local `logmp` and concentration and negative with the late-growth index.
The compact fraction, compact radius, and component-radius ratio show much
weaker binned trends compared with their broad conditional distributions.
These are descriptive scaling relations. They do not establish an
inner--early-MAH or outer--recent-assembly connection.

The held-out halo-mass-sufficiency test separates normalization from profile
shape. Relative to a quadratic relation with concurrent halo mass alone,
adding epoch-local DiffMAH and concentration reduces the direct TNG
`log_mstar_148` RMS by 17.99--28.95% across the five epochs. The corresponding
gains for R50, R80, and R90 are 3.81--8.39%, 3.35--6.51%, and 2.28--5.36%.
Dimensionless enclosed fractions and CoG slopes improve by roughly 1--9%, so
the signal is not only the SHMR normalization. After forcing every decoded
prediction to the measured Mstar within 148 kpc, however, the pooled median
CoG-shape RMS improves only from 0.04866 dex for halo mass alone to 0.04696 dex
with all halo inputs, while the pooled density RMS improves from 0.15127 to
0.15007 dex. Thus additional halo information is clearly useful for total
stellar mass and measurably useful for profile shape, but the recoverable shape
signal in this simple linear relation is second-order rather than dominant.

The family-specific corner plots use physical or base-10-log physical
coordinates and overlay all five epochs. They expose real evolution and broad
parameter covariance, but they also make the known representation issues
visible: the Gompertz size coordinate has an upper-bound pile, the constrained
Sersic--Moffat has smaller boundary subpopulations, and the unrestricted double
Sersic has curved, multimodal component degeneracies and index-boundary piles.
The latter remains an accuracy ceiling rather than a physically interpretable
parameterization.

### Quick test: diversity of mass- and size-normalized CoGs

The direct CoG test defines `q = R/R50` and
`mu(q) = log10[Mstar(<q R50)/Mstar,148]`, so every resolved profile passes
through `(q, mu) = (1, log10 0.5)`. This removes total stellar mass and overall
size before measuring shape diversity. The primary redshift comparison uses
the same 1,745 galaxies whose measured 2--148 kpc CoGs cover `0.7 <= q <= 3`
at every epoch; it never extrapolates compact high-redshift galaxies below the
2 kpc data limit. Diversity is the RMS across log-spaced `q` of half the
population 16--84% width in `mu`.

The inner diversity over `0.7 < q < 1` declines monotonically from 0.01924 dex
at z=2 to 0.00671 dex at z=0.4, a factor of 2.87. The outer diversity over
`1 < q < 3` declines from 0.02783 to 0.01278 dex, a factor of 2.18. Bootstrap
95% intervals at adjacent epochs are mostly or completely separated, and the
same direction appears for common-support selections beginning at q=0.5,
0.8, and 0.9. Thus the measured CoGs become more self-similar toward low
redshift after both mass and R50 are removed, while the outer half retains
more shape diversity than the inner interval at every epoch.

This is population shape scatter, not a noise-deconvolved intrinsic-scatter
estimate. It includes projection and transient structure, and its absolute
amplitude near q=1 is deliberately suppressed because R50 is measured from
the same CoG. The redshift ordering and outer-versus-inner comparison are more
robust than treating the quoted dex values as universal profile parameters.

The companion DiffMAH test applies the same construction to the epoch-local
parameterized peak-mass histories: `q = t/t50` and
`mu(q) = log10[Mpeak(q t50)/Mpeak(t_z)]`. Each earlier-redshift DiffMAH fit uses
only halo history available by that endpoint. The primary comparison uses the
same 1,208 halos with fit support over `0.6 <= q <= 1.3` at all five endpoints;
the 1 Gyr lower fit boundary and each epoch time are treated as hard limits.

The early-history diversity over `0.6 < q < 1` rises from 0.03854 dex for the
z=0.4 endpoint to 0.07121 dex at z=2, a factor of 1.85. This high-redshift
increase is stable to moving the early limit from q=0.5 to 0.8. The late
diversity over `1 < q < 1.3` is smaller, 0.01768--0.02440 dex, and is not
monotonic: its apparent redshift trend changes when the endpoint is moved from
1.2 to 1.5 t50 because progressively different common-support halo samples
are required. Thus normalized DiffMAH histories have a robustly more diverse
pre-half-mass branch at high redshift, but this experiment does not support a
single directional claim for post-half-mass diversity.

## Decision

Keep three distinct products rather than declaring one winner:

1. unrestricted double Sersic as the analytic accuracy ceiling, with its raw
   component parameters barred from physical interpretation;
2. constrained Sersic--Moffat as the best identifiable two-component summary;
3. log-radius Gompertz as the stable minimal one-component summary.

Carry both log-CoG and absolute-CoG fits when density or R90 accuracy matters,
because their trade is resolved and objective-dependent. Do not adopt a
per-galaxy smooth-in-time parameter law at five-epoch sampling. Use the Stage 4
relations only to formulate and test radial assembly hypotheses in subsequent
work; do not graduate them as a portable halo--CoG model.

## Stage 5 — predeclared search for a simpler analytic family

The final task in this branch asks whether the six-parameter unrestricted
double-Sersic accuracy ceiling can be approached by a stable analytic CoG with
fewer per-galaxy coordinates. The primary target is four total parameters,
including `log_mstar_148`; five are an allowed fallback. A candidate may use at
most four shared dimensionless constants and one shared coefficient describing
linear evolution with standardized cosmic time. The radial equation itself
must remain the same at all five epochs. Direct evaluation may use standard
special functions, but not numerical radial integration or stored radial basis
arrays.

The search proceeds from the mass- and R50-normalized CoG geometry. PCA is used
only to expose recurrent residual modes and to set an empirical compression
reference; it is not an eligible analytic answer. Candidate equations are
generated through three controlled routes: monotonic symbolic regression in
log radius, sparse selection among monotonic analytic transition functions,
and one-kernel corrections to the best existing simple families. The first
explicit candidates are a four-parameter generalized Moffat/Burr cumulative
profile and its five-parameter regularized-beta generalization. Both have
independently adjustable inner and outer asymptotic slopes without assigning
two degenerate physical components.

Every proposed equation must be positive, monotonic, normalized exactly at
148.2 kpc, finite across the fitted domain, recoverable from synthetic data,
and stable under separated starts, radial jackknifes, and dense-grid derivative
checks. Shared expression choices and any epoch coefficient are learned on
galaxy-level training folds, with all epochs of one galaxy kept in the same
fold. Held-out galaxies still receive ordinary per-CoG profile fits; the folds
test whether the selected equation is a population-general form.

Two result tiers are predeclared. A replacement candidate must have no more
than 10% larger median full-range and 5--30 kpc CoG RMS than unrestricted
double Sersic at every epoch, no more than 10% worse density and R50/R80/R90
errors, and substantially better conditioning and boundary behavior. A useful
compression must beat the four-parameter PCA(3) reconstruction in CoG accuracy
at four or more epochs, match or improve its density and outer-size recovery,
and show no systematic failure in the population QA. Failure to reach either
tier is a valid result.

Before any full calculation, the complete fitting, metrics, and minimum visual
path must pass on the established 90-profile mass-stratified sample in less
than one minute. Only Pareto-improving candidates proceed to the complete
16,900-profile atlas. Promising candidates receive the standard average CoGs
in halo- and stellar-mass bins, stellar-mass planes, mass--size relations,
density and radial-mass recovery, residual CDFs, ten worst cases, and numerical
conditioning diagnostics. Search rounds stop after a declared winner or three
successive rounds without an improved accuracy--stability frontier.

### Stage 5 implementation and measured result

The first search round tested the predeclared generalized-Moffat/Burr and
regularized-beta cumulative profiles, plus fixed- and free-width warped-logit
transitions. The four-parameter Burr form was stable but could not recover the
outer sizes. The five-parameter regularized-beta form was more accurate, but
its shape coordinates accumulated at their bounds and were poorly
conditioned. These familiar one-transition forms therefore did not improve the
accuracy--stability frontier.

Their median residuals showed a repeated change of curvature in log radius.
The second round encoded that residual geometry directly with a constrained
polynomial inside a logistic cumulative:

\[
M_*(<R)=M_{*,148}\frac{\sigma[g(x)]}{\sigma[g(x_{148})]},
\qquad x=\ln(R/R_s),
\]

\[
g(x)=a x+b x^2+c x^3,\qquad
c=e^{p_c}>0,\qquad
a=e^{p_m}+\frac{b^2}{3c}.
\]

The constraint gives

\[
g'(x)=e^{p_m}+3c\left(x+\frac{b}{3c}\right)^2>0,
\]

so the CoG is monotonic for every allowed parameter value. The fitted
coordinates are `log_mstar_148`, `log_scale_radius`,
`log_derivative_margin`, `quadratic_warp`, and `log_cubic_warp`: five total
per CoG. The equation contains no learned global constants, stored basis, or
epoch-specific term; the same form was used unchanged at all five epochs. A
shared epoch coefficient was not introduced because independent per-CoG
coordinates would absorb it, making that coefficient unidentifiable in this
representation-only experiment.

All eleven concrete candidates passed the complete 90-profile path in 30.98
seconds, including fits, metrics, and figures. Three cubic-logit objective
variants then proceeded to all 16,900 profiles. For the log-CoG fit, the median
full-CoG RMS across epochs is 0.00249 dex, compared with 0.00263 dex for
unrestricted double Sersic and 0.00357 dex for PCA(3). Its median 5--30 kpc
CoG RMS is 0.00241 dex, compared with 0.00233 dex for double Sersic. It is not a
uniform replacement: at z=0.4 its median R90 error is 0.02195 dex, compared
with 0.01893 dex for double Sersic, and at z=2 its density RMS is 0.20625 dex,
compared with 0.17962 dex.

The 90 development profiles were then excluded from a separate population
validation calculation, leaving 16,810 galaxy--epoch profiles. The retained
metrics are effectively unchanged: the log-fit cubic logit has 0.00249 dex
median full-CoG RMS, and the absolute-fit version has 0.00322 dex. The same
replacement failures and useful-compression pass are recovered. Because every
profile still receives its own coordinates, this checks that the selected
equation generalizes across galaxies; it is not a halo-to-CoG prediction test.

Fitting the same equation to absolute cumulative-mass errors changes the
trade. Its median full-CoG RMS across epochs is 0.00322 dex, worse than both
the 0.00263-dex double-Sersic reference and the log-fit cubic-logit result, but
its median density RMS is 0.07206 dex and median R90 error is 0.00968 dex,
better than PCA(3)'s 0.07560-dex density RMS and 0.01709-dex R90 error. It beats
PCA(3) in full-CoG RMS at four of five epochs and passes the predeclared
numerical useful-compression gate. A hybrid log-plus-absolute objective lands
between those endpoints but does not pass either numerical tier.

The new equation has no fit failures or parameters within one percent of a
bound in the complete sample. Under the log objective, its median normalized
Jacobian minimum-singular-value ratio across epochs is 0.00948, compared with
0.00078 for unrestricted double Sersic; larger is better, so the local
conditioning is about twelve times stronger at the median epoch. Alternating
radii move the decoded CoG by 0.00078--0.00416 dex in the median 90-profile
test, while excluding radii below 5 kpc moves it by 0.02151 dex. The latter is
not an optimizer failure: it shows that the inner measurements are needed to
identify the complete shape.

The visual gate is qualified rather than clean. Average CoGs in halo- and
stellar-mass bins and the stellar-mass and size planes support the compact
family. However, the maximum CDF separation for the fitted 100--148 kpc shell
mass has a median of 0.05651 across epochs for the absolute-fit cubic logit,
compared with 0.02515 for PCA(3). Unrestricted double Sersic is also poor on
this diagnostic, with 0.09970, confirming that tiny coherent differences of
the cumulative curves remain scientifically visible after differencing.

### Stage 5 decision

Do not replace unrestricted double Sersic as the analytic accuracy ceiling:
none of the candidates satisfies every predeclared CoG, density, and size
limit at every epoch. Retain the five-parameter asymmetric monotonic
cubic-logit equation as a useful, identifiable analytic compression. Use its
log-CoG fit when cumulative-profile fidelity is primary and its absolute-CoG
fit when local density and outer sizes are primary. Do not claim that it solves
the outer-shell population discrepancy, and do not interpret its warp
coordinates as physical galaxy components without a separate association
test.

The search loop stops here because the second round established a new
accuracy--stability frontier and answered the branch's final question: one
parameter can be removed from unrestricted double Sersic while retaining very
similar cumulative-CoG accuracy, but no tested four-parameter analytic family
does so, and the fifth parameter does not eliminate objective-dependent local
structure errors.

## Stage 6 — predeclared halo information in the cubic-logit shape

This follow-up asks a descriptive question: after current halo mass is known,
which DiffMAH and `c_200c` coordinates contain reproducible information about
the four non-mass cubic-logit coordinates? It does not attempt to build a
portable halo-to-CoG emulator or to explain all individual profile scatter.
The primary target is the log-CoG cubic-logit fit; the absolute-CoG fit and the
measured mass-normalized CoG provide representation checks. `log_mstar_148` is
never a regression target. When parameter predictions are decoded, each
galaxy retains its fitted `log_mstar_148`, so the evaluation isolates profile
shape from the stellar--halo mass relation.

The primary baseline is a quadratic relation with concurrent halo mass. In
five galaxy-level folds, compare that baseline with four nested additions:

1. the four epoch-local DiffMAH coordinates alone;
2. concurrent `c_200c` alone;
3. all DiffMAH coordinates plus `c_200c` in a linear residual relation;
4. the same complete relation with one centered quadratic term per predictor,
   but no predictor interactions.

The additive quadratic relation is retained only if it improves decoded
full-CoG RMS over the linear complete relation by at least 2% at three or more
epochs without worsening density or R80/R90 at three or more epochs. Otherwise
the linear relation remains the interpretable reference. No higher polynomial,
interaction expansion, symbolic search, or dense emulator is allowed in this
stage.

For the selected complete relation, use 200 galaxy bootstraps and 32
mass-conditioned shuffles of the DiffMAH and concentration blocks separately.
Report held-out residual variance and RMS for every raw coordinate, fold and
bootstrap sign stability, predictor conditioning, parameter-bound incidence,
and decoded CoG, 5--30 kpc, density, R50/R80/R90, and radial-jackknife behavior.
Compare predictor-induced radial responses decoded through the cubic-logit
coordinates with responses fitted directly to the measured normalized CoGs.
Agreement supports a representation-robust association; disagreement bars
raw-coordinate interpretation.

The visual record must include the coefficient and null-control summaries,
decoded radial responses, average CoGs in concurrent halo- and stellar-mass
bins, stellar-mass and mass--size planes, aperture/annulus/outskirt CDFs, and
the ten largest decoded CoG failures. The complete calculation and minimum QA
must first pass on 90 mass-stratified galaxies in less than one minute. A
positive result requires a held-out decoded improvement over current halo mass,
survival of the corresponding real-input gain against both shuffled blocks,
and consistent radial response signs across folds, bootstraps, and the direct
CoG representation. Any inner--early-MAH or outer--recent-assembly pattern is
treated only as a tested statistical association, not established physics.

### Stage 6 implementation and gate

`stage6.py` aligns the 2,365 quality-clean, five-epoch halo histories with the
independently fitted cubic-logit coordinates and assigns galaxies, rather than
individual epochs, to five held-out folds. Within every training fold it first
fits the four shape coordinates as quadratic functions of concurrent halo
mass, removes the same mass trend from each halo predictor, and then fits the
leftover coordinate variation. Therefore the complete relation asks whether
DiffMAH and concentration describe profile shape beyond concurrent halo mass;
it does not allow either the measured shape or another epoch of the same
galaxy to enter its held-out prediction.

The complete 90-galaxy path, including 40 bootstraps, eight shuffles, decoding,
and all QA figures, took 17.58 seconds and passed the one-minute gate. The
additive-quadratic relation improved decoded full-CoG RMS by at least 2% at
only two of five epochs and was simultaneously nonworse in density, R80, and
R90 at only one epoch. It was therefore rejected as predeclared, and the
complete linear relation was fixed before the 2,365-galaxy calculation. The
full calculation, with 200 bootstraps and 32 shuffles, took 36.31 seconds.

### Stage 6 result

There is measurable but weak halo-assembly information in the four fitted
shape coordinates after concurrent halo mass is known. Across the five
epochs, the complete linear relation explains 3.0--7.3% of the mass-residual
variance in log scale radius, 2.6--7.4% in log derivative margin, 2.3--9.5% in
quadratic warp, and 1.0--2.4% in log cubic warp; zero would mean no held-out
gain over the halo-mass relation. Decoding these predictions reduces the
median full-CoG RMS relative to halo mass alone by 4.19%, 6.25%, 3.21%, and
0.67% at z=0.4, 0.7, 1.0, and 1.5, respectively, but worsens it by 0.12% at
z=2. The corresponding median 5--30 kpc CoG RMS improves by 3.34--5.12% at all
five epochs. For example, at z=0.7 the median full-CoG RMS falls from 0.05784
dex for concurrent halo mass alone to 0.05422 dex when DiffMAH and `c_200c` are
added. This is evidence for assembly-conditioned shape at the population
level, but the improvement is small compared with the remaining individual
scatter.

The gain is carried almost entirely by DiffMAH in this representation.
DiffMAH alone explains as much mass-residual coordinate variance as the
complete relation to the quoted precision, while concentration alone explains
at most 1.9% for any coordinate and epoch. Shuffling DiffMAH while retaining
real concentration returns the median decoded CoG RMS to approximately the
halo-mass-only result; shuffling concentration while retaining real DiffMAH
leaves approximately the complete-relation result. This does not contradict
earlier concentration gains for other target layers: here total stellar mass
is held fixed and only the four cubic-logit shape coordinates are tested.

The broad association is not an artifact of one fitting objective. The
standardized coefficient patterns from the log-CoG and absolute-CoG fits
correlate by 0.954--0.986 across epochs, where one would mean identical
patterns, and 85--100% of their coefficient signs agree. Decoding the
parameter response and comparing it with a relation fitted directly to the
measured mass-normalized CoG gives a median radial-pattern correlation of
0.896. However, only 69.6% of radial signs agree across every predictor,
epoch, and radius. In particular, some late-growth, concentration, and
`logmp` responses change or lose their radial interpretation. The result
supports a weak aggregate assembly--shape association, not a unique physical
meaning for each analytic coordinate.

The mass-conditioned five-predictor design is numerically well conditioned:
its median matrix condition number is 1.77--1.98 across epochs, where one is
ideal. Thus ordinary predictor collinearity is not driving the result. The
underlying cubic-logit fits also retain the Stage 5 radial-jackknife result:
using alternating radial samples moves the decoded profile by 0.00078 and
0.00416 dex in the median, while excluding all measurements below 5 kpc moves
it by 0.02151 dex. The first two checks are much smaller than the
0.0420--0.0558 dex median held-out CoG RMS of the complete halo relation; the
inner cut is not. The broad association is stable to alternating measurements,
but a version that does not observe the inner profile would require a new fit
and should not inherit these coefficients.

Median and percentile improvements also hide a sparse extrapolation problem.
The complete relation lowers the 90th-percentile CoG RMS at every epoch and
the 99th percentile at four of five epochs, but its maximum held-out CoG RMS is
0.61--2.90 dex, compared with 0.27--0.35 dex for halo mass alone. The largest
failure at every epoch is the same galaxy, identifier 1365, whose epoch-local
DiffMAH slopes lie on their allowed extremes; several other worst cases have
the same predictor-bound pattern. A DiffMAH slope touches an allowed extreme
for 29.9--34.5% of galaxies at an individual epoch and for 83.2% at least once,
so a boundary value alone is common and not a sufficient failure flag; the
problem is the rare multivariate extreme passed through an unbounded linear
relation. No predicted cubic-logit coordinate itself was clipped, so this is
genuine linear extrapolation through an unusual halo history rather than a
decoder-bound artifact.

### Stage 6 decision

Answer the halo-information question with a qualified yes: after total
stellar mass and concurrent halo mass are controlled, DiffMAH contains a
small, held-out, representation-robust amount of information about CoG shape.
The present test finds negligible incremental `c_200c` information for this
specific shape representation. Retain the complete linear relation only as a
descriptive population diagnostic. Do not use it as an individual-galaxy
emulator, do not interpret the four coordinate coefficients as unique physical
channels, and do not record an inner--early-MAH or outer--recent-assembly
connection as established physics. A portable predictor would need an
explicit treatment of residual scatter and bounded or robust behavior for
unusual DiffMAH histories.

## Stage 7 — predeclared assembly information in scale-free CoG diversity

### Scientific question

Stage 6 used four analytic cubic-logit coordinates and therefore mixed
residual size variation with residual radial-shape variation. Stage 7 asks the
more specific HongShao question: after both total stellar mass and overall
size are removed, does halo assembly predict the remaining galaxy-to-galaxy
diversity of the CoG?

For galaxy (i), normalized radius (x=R/R_{50,i}), and epoch (z), define

\[
g_i(x,z)=\log_{10}\!\left[
\frac{M_{\star,i}(<xR_{50,i},z)}{M_{\star,i}(<148\,{\rm kpc},z)}
\right].
\]

The measured CoG supplies both (M_{\star}(<148\,{\rm kpc})) and R50. This is
an information audit, not a portable prediction, so using the measured R50 is
intentional: it removes horizontal scale before asking about shape. Every
profile is anchored at (g_i(1,z)=\log_{10}(0.5)); exclude that fixed point
from every information metric.

The primary target is the measured normalized CoG itself, not an analytic
parameter. A fold-local PCA(3) reconstruction is only a representation check:
learn its mean and basis on the training galaxies, predict the three scores,
decode the full normalized CoG, and judge the decoded curve. A direct radial
linear relation is the scientific reference. No conclusion may be based only
on PCA scores or fitted coordinates.

### Sample and radial support

Never extrapolate beyond the measured 2--148 kpc CoG. A support-only audit,
performed before any halo fit, found that the assembly-clean sample cannot
reach 90% five-epoch coverage at a scientifically useful radius below R50:
compact high-redshift galaxies place the measured inner boundary too close to
R50. Retain the existing normalized-diversity definition of
(0.7\leq R/R_{50}\leq3) as the primary common-support interval, yielding
1,257 galaxies with complete support at all five epochs. Use
(0.85\leq R/R_{50}\leq3), yielding 1,743 common-support galaxies, as the
larger-sample sensitivity check. Freeze both samples before inspecting halo
associations. Give the inner and outer sides of R50 equal total weight so the
denser radial grid does not choose the answer.

Use five galaxy-level held-out folds; all five epochs of a galaxy remain in
the same fold. First run the complete path on 90 galaxies stratified by
concurrent halo mass and require less than one minute before any full-sample
fit.

### Nested information tests

At each epoch, fit the average normalized profile as a quadratic function of
concurrent halo mass. Compare its held-out prediction with four residual
relations:

1. the four epoch-local DiffMAH coordinates, linearly;
2. concurrent `c_200c`, linearly;
3. DiffMAH plus `c_200c`, linearly;
4. the complete set with one additive quadratic term per predictor and no
   interactions, as a nonlinear sensitivity check rather than a selected
   production model.

Mass-condition every halo predictor inside the training fold before fitting
the residual relation. The primary baseline uses concurrent halo mass alone.
A stricter secondary baseline adds the measured `log_mstar_148` and log R50 to
the training-fold design. Although amplitude and radial scale have already
been divided out, this stricter comparison asks whether any assembly signal is
merely inherited from residual mass--size--shape correlations.

For each relation, report the held-out fraction of baseline diversity
explained,

\[
R^2_{\rm diversity}=1-
\frac{\sum_i\|g_i-\widehat g_{i,{\rm relation}}\|^2}
     {\sum_i\|g_i-\widehat g_{i,{\rm baseline}}\|^2},
\]

separately over (0.7\!<x<1), (1<x<3), and the equally weighted combination
of the two. Positive values mean that halo assembly predicts part of the
individual normalized-profile variation remaining after the baseline; they do
not measure the accuracy of the average CoG. Also evaluate independent coarse
annular mass fractions so repeated enclosed-mass information cannot create a
false positive.

Use 200 galaxy bootstraps and 32 mass-conditioned block shuffles for the full
sample, with 40 bootstraps and eight shuffles in the demo. Shuffle DiffMAH and
concentration separately while preserving each block's internal correlations.
Record predictor conditioning, fold and bootstrap sign stability, the
fraction of DiffMAH slopes at fitted bounds, direct-versus-PCA agreement,
the larger-support sensitivity result, and the ten largest held-out normalized
CoG errors.

### Decision rule and visual record

Call the information material only if a real halo block explains at least 5%
of held-out inner or outer normalized-shape variance at three or more epochs,
exceeds the 95th percentile of the corresponding shuffled control, and has the
same conclusion for the direct and fold-local PCA(3) representations. Call a
smaller but shuffle-separated result detectable but minor. Otherwise record no
demonstrated assembly information at the present precision. A signal may be
radially localized; do not require both inner and outer regions to pass.

The minimum figures are: measured normalized-CoG diversity and coverage;
radial--redshift maps of explained diversity; inner and outer explained
diversity versus redshift with bootstrap and shuffle intervals; measured and
predicted residual profiles split by mass-conditioned DiffMAH quantile;
conditional radial responses with bootstrap bands; CDF residuals of normalized
aperture and annular masses; direct-versus-PCA and primary-versus-strict
baseline comparisons; and the ten worst held-out profiles. Interpret any
radial assembly pattern as a statistical association, not established
inner--early-MAH or outer--recent-assembly physics.

### Result

The complete 90-galaxy path took 16.97 seconds, below the predeclared
one-minute gate. The full calculation then used 1,257 galaxies, five held-out
galaxy folds, 200 bootstraps, and 32 mass-conditioned shuffles. Predictor
condition numbers are only 1.84--1.98, so the result is not produced by a
nearly singular halo design. However, 33--41% of galaxies at each epoch have
at least one fitted DiffMAH slope at its allowed boundary; this remains a
warning against interpreting individual DiffMAH coordinates literally.

The average normalized CoG in concurrent halo-mass bins is already reproduced
by the halo-mass-only baseline. Adding DiffMAH changes those mean curves by
less than their visible line width. The information measured below is
therefore information about the *diversity around the average normalized
profile*, not information required to recover the average profile itself.

The held-out fraction of the halo-mass-baseline diversity explained by the
complete halo relation is:

| z | linear: 0.7--1 R50 | linear: 1--3 R50 | additive quadratic: 0.7--1 R50 | additive quadratic: 1--3 R50 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.4 | 2.67% | 3.20% | 6.81% | 4.33% |
| 0.7 | 0.35% | 0.17% | 1.72% | 1.82% |
| 1.0 | 5.40% | 0.28% | 6.45% | 0.26% |
| 1.5 | 6.20% | 1.34% | 7.31% | 2.93% |
| 2.0 | 1.77% | 0.59% | 2.50% | -0.22% |

The linear relation is shuffle-separated at four epochs but crosses the 5%
material threshold inside R50 at only z=1 and z=1.5. It is therefore a
detectable but minor association under the predeclared rule. The additive
quadratic relation crosses 5% inside R50 at z=0.4, 1, and 1.5, exceeds the
95th percentile of the shuffled-DiffMAH control, and gives the same decision
when the direct curve is replaced by a fold-local PCA(3) representation. It
therefore passes the formal material-association rule, but only for a modest,
radially localized, nonlinear signal. At least 92.7% of the inner diversity
and at least 95.7% of the outer diversity remains unexplained at every epoch.

Three safeguards clarify the signal:

- Adding measured stellar mass and measured R50 to the baseline leaves the
  primary inner result above 5% at z=0.4, 1, and 1.5: 9.62%, 6.35%, and 7.39%
  of that stricter baseline's remaining diversity is explained.
- Replacing cumulative radial points by independent coarse annular mass
  fractions also leaves more than 5% explained inside R50 at the same three
  epochs: 6.55%, 6.65%, and 6.41%. The result is not created by repeatedly
  counting the same enclosed mass.
- On the larger 1,743-galaxy sample with the narrower 0.85--3 R50 support, the
  quadratic inner result is 3.59%, 4.97%, 6.77%, 4.88%, and 1.81% from z=0.4
  to z=2. The sign and minor-to-moderate amplitude persist, but only z=1
  crosses 5%. Calling the effect "material" therefore depends on measuring
  profile diversity down to 0.7 R50 rather than only close to R50.

DiffMAH supplies essentially all of the detected halo information.
Concentration alone explains between -0.15% and 0.44% of inner diversity and
between -0.25% and 0.04% of outer diversity; shuffling concentration while
retaining DiffMAH leaves the quadratic result almost unchanged. The radial
responses and DiffMAH-quantile residual profiles vary with epoch and do not
support a single monotonic inner--early-MAH or outer--recent-assembly story.

The quadratic relation is not an accurate individual-galaxy emulator. Its
equal-inner/outer held-out profile error has epoch-dependent median values of
0.0068--0.0134 dex, 90th percentiles of 0.0171--0.0296 dex, and a maximum of
0.0576 dex. Its normalized aperture and annular-mass CDFs remain noticeably
narrower than the measured distributions. It introduces a non-monotonic
segment in only 0.016% of galaxy--epoch profiles, so numerical pathology is
rare, but it still predicts only a small part of the observed diversity.

### Decision

Record evidence that DiffMAH contains a modest nonlinear association with
mass- and R50-normalized CoG diversity, localized mainly to 0.7--1 R50 at
z=0.4--1.5. Concurrent halo mass is sufficient for the average normalized
CoG, and DiffMAH is not demonstrated to be generally necessary for the outer
profile or for precise individual profiles. Treat the additive-quadratic
relation as an information diagnostic, not a portable halo--CoG relation, and
do not assign its epoch-dependent pattern a causal assembly interpretation.

## Stage 8 — predeclared cubic-logit mathematical reference and promotion

### Name and scope

Name the five-parameter Stage 5 candidate the **cubic-logit profile**. Use
`cubic_logit` as its Python family name. This describes the construction
directly and makes no claim that the coordinates represent distinct physical
components.

Write the infinite-aperture enclosed fraction as

\[
F(R)=\sigma[P(x)],\qquad x=\ln(R/R_{50}),
\]

\[
P(x)=a x+b x^2+c x^3,\qquad
a=m+\frac{b^2}{3c},\quad m>0,\quad c>0.
\]

The completed-square derivative

\[
P'(x)=m+3c\left(x+\frac{b}{3c}\right)^2>0
\]

must remain the defining monotonicity constraint. The five intrinsic profile
parameters are total projected mass, total half-mass radius, derivative margin
`m`, asymmetry `b`, and cubic curvature `c`. HongShao's measured-aperture
convention requires a separate helper that takes `Mstar(<R_ap)` and divides by
`F(R_ap)`; the aperture mass must not be mislabeled as the infinite total.

### Mathematical audit

Derive and verify:

1. the projected surface-density profile from
   `dM(<R)/(2 pi R dR)`, its logarithmic slope, its inner and outer
   asymptotes, and the number and location of density maxima;
2. the exact meaning of R50 and a closed expression for every enclosed radius,
   including R20 and R80, by reducing the monotonic cubic to a depressed cubic;
3. circular and elliptical 2-D rendering, including the axis-ratio
   normalization and finite-pixel convergence;
4. the normalized circular Fourier transform as an order-zero Hankel
   transform, its zero-frequency and low-frequency limits, and whether an
   elementary closed form exists;
5. Gaussian and Moffat PSF transfer functions, flux conservation under
   convolution, and a numerical convolution demonstration.

The analysis must state plainly if a useful CoG interpolation has an
undesirable extrapolation outside the measured 2--148 kpc range. In
particular, measure the central density turnover for all 16,900 Stage 5
galaxy--epoch fits instead of judging it from a few examples.

### Reusable interface and checks

Create `hongshao/cubic_logit.py` with documented functions for the polynomial
warp, cumulative fraction, aperture-normalized CoG, projected density,
enclosed radius, 2-D rendering, the normalized Fourier transform, and Gaussian
and Moffat PSF transfer functions. Route the Stage 5 experiment implementation
through the promoted cumulative-fraction function so the tested candidate and
the library family cannot drift apart.

Write the checks first. Require exact monotonicity on a dense radial grid;
R50 exact to floating-point precision; R20/R80 substitution errors below
1e-10 in enclosed fraction; numerical density integration consistent with the
cumulative fraction; circular and elliptical images conserving flux as pixel
resolution increases; Fourier value one at zero frequency; the Fourier
small-frequency expansion consistent with the numerically measured second
moment; analytic Gaussian and Moffat transfer functions consistent with
directly transformed PSF images; and PSF convolution conserving total flux.

Run the complete mathematical path first on the 90-profile development sample
and require less than one minute before applying the population audit to all
16,900 fitted galaxy--epoch profiles. Produce PNG and PDF figures showing the
equation and parameter responses; cumulative and density profiles with R20,
R50, R80, and density maxima; fitted-population turnover and extrapolated-mass
distributions; 2-D circular and elliptical renderings; Fourier transforms;
and Gaussian/Moffat-convolved profiles. The resolved-range QA must compare the
promoted implementation with the stored Stage 5 predictions and data rather
than refitting them.

### Implementation and measured result

The checks were written before the reusable implementation. The complete
90-profile path, including mathematical properties and figures, took 15.85
seconds and passed the one-minute gate. The subsequent audit of all 16,900
galaxy--epoch fits took 21.50 seconds. The promoted implementation reproduces
the stored Stage 5 predictions over 2--148 kpc to a maximum difference of
1.78e-15 dex, so promotion did not change the fitted model.

The exact enclosed-radius solution follows from a depressed cubic with one
real root because `m/c > 0`. R20, R50, and R80 therefore need no numerical
root finder. The projected density and its logarithmic slope are analytic,
while density extrema require a one-dimensional numerical root search. A
circular or elliptical 2-D image is a direct surface-density evaluation with
the usual `1/q` axis-ratio normalization.

The normalized circular Fourier transform is an order-zero Hankel integral;
no elementary expression is known. Fixed quadrature in log radius gives
`T(0)=1` and the expected low-frequency second-moment expansion. Gaussian and
Moffat PSFs have analytic transfer functions, so a circular convolution is
performed by multiplication in Fourier space. Elliptical profiles or PSFs use
the rendered image and a two-dimensional FFT. Increasing the representative
profile's quadrature order from 1,024 to 4,096 changes the transform by no more
than 6.23e-4 over `0 <= k R50 <= 30`.

The infinite-radius continuation has two measured limitations:

- Every allowed cubic-logit profile has projected density approaching zero at
  the exact center. The median global density maximum is 0.69--0.73 kpc over
  the five epochs; 16,896 of 16,900 fits have that maximum below 2 kpc, and
  only 5 are rising at 2 kpc. The defect is almost always unresolved by the
  2--148 kpc fitting support, but it makes the family unsuitable as a physical
  central profile or nonnegative spherical deprojection.
- The median extrapolation from measured `Mstar(<148 kpc)` to infinite total
  is only 0.00563 dex, and 84% of fits require no more than 0.0241 dex.
  However, the 99th percentile is 0.401 dex and the maximum is 0.771 dex.
  Infinite totals from the extreme tail are not constrained by the measured
  CoG and must not be treated as reliable stellar masses.

Only 36 of 16,900 fits have more than one projected-density maximum, and only
23 fail to decrease throughout the measured 2--148 kpc interval. Thus the
resolved behavior is normally simple despite the nonphysical inward
continuation. Full equations, API examples, PSF conventions, and cautions are
recorded in `doc/cubic_logit_profile.md`.

**Decision:** promote cubic-logit as a precise, identifiable projected-CoG
interpolator with exact characteristic radii and convenient rendering. Use
the aperture-normalized interface for HongShao. Do not use it as a globally
physical central density law, a spherical 3-D profile, or an unconstrained
infinite-total estimator.
