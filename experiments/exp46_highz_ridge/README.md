# exp46 — the high-z ridge: is the target real?

The program's top outstanding deliverable gap. The adopted product's
inner-vs-outer stellar mass planes (qa tier 2b) degrade monotonically
with redshift and break at z >= 1.5 — centered energy/split-half-floor
2.2 / 2.6 / 2.8 / 3.5 / **4.8** across z = 0.4 -> 2.0 for the
deterministic kernel (2.6-2.8 at z=2 even with the adopted stochastic
layer), against the 1.0-1.1 the same product reaches at z = 0.4.

## What the 2026-08-12 discussion established

Three phenomena had been sharing the name "high-z ridge error". They
behave differently and must not be argued about together:

- **(A) the plane residual** — an *under-dispersion* failure. The truth's
  population gets MORE diverse toward high z (scatter on
  log M\*(<30 kpc) vs log M\*(30-50 kpc): 0.170 -> 0.338 dex; on
  M\*(<30) vs M\*(50-100): 0.204 -> 0.539) while the model's gets LESS
  (0.077 -> 0.067) and the model's slope collapses +1.41 -> +1.07 — its
  z=2 galaxies are near-homologous scaled copies. Monotone from z=0.4
  with a knee at 1.5, not a switch at 1.5.
- **(B) per-object accuracy, which does NOT degrade at high z** —
  M\*(<5 kpc) bias -11.7% at z=0.4 vs -8.0% at z=2; held-out pinned
  shape 18.2% -> 15.4%. Whatever fails at high z, it is not per-galaxy
  accuracy; it is a population statistic.
- **(C) the size story** (exp45 follow-up 4) — an R50-only offset at
  z <= 1.5 (the inner-mass deficit in size units) and a genuine,
  separate OUTER over-extension at z = 2.0 (R80/R90 +0.116/+0.112 vs
  R50 +0.087). (A) is "too uniform"; (C) is "too big".

**The fit-scope explanation is falsified for (A)**, twice, from logs
already on disk (their truth columns are byte-identical, so the
comparison is like-for-like):

| z=2.0, deterministic 1ch-mof | model slope / scatter | E/floor (centered) |
|---|---|---|
| z <= 1.5 fit — z=2 is extrapolation | +1.07 / 0.067 | 7.2 (**4.8**) |
| z <= 2.0 fit — z=2 is IN the loss | +1.07 / 0.062 | 6.1 (**4.8**) |

Across every epoch the fit scope moves the plane score by <= 0.2.
Fitting the epoch does not fix the epoch. Second, independent
falsification: the exp37 statistical emulator fits every epoch
separately (z=1.5 and z=2.0 are in-sample for it) and still breaks at
exactly those epochs — its draws go from 0.5-0.7x floor at z <= 1.0 to
1.5-2.6x at z >= 1.5.

**exp40's dissipative-era finding is correctly scoped**: every
measurement behind it concerns the mean profile's INNER MASS. It does
not predict a 5x scatter deficit, so it does not address (A). Its
natural target is (C), and that link has never been measured.

**The accepted leading cause of (A) is the deposit machinery.** With
mu = 1.52, sigma = 0.24 the efficiency window peaks at z ~ 3.6 (half-max
z ~ 2.6-4.8), so at z = 2 (t ~ 3.3 Gyr) every parcel carrying budget
arrived within a factor ~2 in cosmic time — nearly the same retained
fraction f_c, birth radii on one steep r_c ∝ t_i^4 law. The model's
population diversity is generated by the SPREAD of arrival times inside
a galaxy, and at z = 2 there is barely any spread. Corroborated three
independent ways: exp45 follow-up 1 (sigma is the ONLY lever on
cross-epoch coherence; q and log_rc inert), exp42 (the real-MAH refit
picks sigma = 0.52, g = 2.43 and lands its largest gains at exactly
z = 1.5 and z = 2.0), exp45 scoreboard (the real-MAH row is one of only
two partial coherence exceptions).

## Why this experiment comes FIRST

A fourth explanation was named in the same discussion and has never been
bounded: the failure is **not the kernel's alone**. Two model families
that share only "predicts from a low-dimensional halo feature vector"
break at the same epochs by a similar factor. And qa's own resolution
disclaimer bites at z=2: it declares 2-5 kpc marginally resolved
(`RMIN_KPC = 5.0`), the CoG grid starts at 2.0 kpc — but **the truth's
median R50 at z = 2 is 3.0 kpc**, inside that band, and the "missing
compact tail" (log R50 < 0.3) lies BELOW the grid, reached by
`enclosed_radius`'s log-log extrapolation. The split-half floor is built
from the truth, so it contains whatever object-level noise the high-z
progenitor measurements carry, and a noiseless model is scored for
failing to reproduce it.

So before spending a fitting campaign on widening the efficiency window,
answer the cheaper question: **how much of the z >= 1.5 target is real
structure a model should chase?**

### A correction to the discussion's framing, recorded before building

The discussion floated measuring "how much of the truth's high-z
diversity is predictable from halo features" (e.g. by twin-matching
galaxies in feature space). **That instrument does not work on this
metric and the idea is dropped.** `plane_energy` compares two
POPULATIONS; it is blind to which halo produced which galaxy. A "model"
that emits the truth's own galaxies in random order scores at the floor,
so any feature-conditioned resampling scores ~1.0 by construction
regardless of how predictive the features are. (Same family of blindness
as exp44's "single-epoch metrics are structurally blind to cross-epoch
coherence".)

The consequence sharpens the experiment: because the metric is
pairing-blind, a model can always be made to match a wide target by
injecting scatter. So the question is not "is the spread reachable" —
it is **"is the spread REAL"**, i.e. should the model be built to
reproduce it at all.

## Pre-registered readouts (fixed before running)

No fitting anywhere in stage 1. Truth CoGs and halo features come from
`exp32 outputs/population.npz` (n=2397, 5 epochs, 24 radii,
2.0-148.22 kpc); model CoGs for stage 1d from the adopted theta
(exp40 `latestart.npz[theta_z15]`).

**1a — where the scatter lives.** The plane's transverse scatter split
by inner-mass bin, per epoch. Decides whether 0.338 dex is a bulk
property of the population or is carried by a tail.

**1b — the resolution audit.** Per epoch: the fraction of TRUTH galaxies
with R50 below 2.0 kpc (off the CoG grid — size obtained by
extrapolation) and below 5.0 kpc (`RMIN_KPC`, marginally resolved); the
distribution of the annulus masses and the implied stellar-particle
counts (TNG300-1), giving a shot-noise floor in dex to set against the
measured 0.34-0.54.

**1c — the near-empty progenitor census.** Per epoch, log M\*(<148 kpc)
relative to the same galaxy at z=0.4; counts at >1, >1.5, >2 dex down
(exp37's broken-progenitor criterion was >2 dex, and masked 2 galaxies).

**1d — the trimmed re-score (the decisive one).** Re-score tier 2b and
tier 2d for the adopted deterministic kernel over NESTED subsamples:
all -> drop >2 dex-down progenitors -> drop R50 < 2 kpc -> drop
R50 < 5 kpc. Report how E/floor moves at every epoch.

**The mandatory control for 1d**: trimming shrinks n, and a smaller
sample has a LARGER split-half floor, which lowers E/floor mechanically
even for an unchanged model. Every trim is therefore paired with a
RANDOM trim of the same size, and only the difference between the
targeted trim and the random trim is interpreted.

### How each outcome will be read (also fixed in advance)

- Targeted trims move E/floor at z=2 from 4.8 to near the z=0.4 value,
  beyond the random-trim control -> the high-z "failure" is largely an
  ill-resolved / near-empty tail. Re-scope the success criterion; the
  machinery campaign is not justified by this metric.
- Targeted trims barely move it beyond the control -> the deficit is in
  the well-measured bulk, the target is real, and the efficiency-window
  campaign is justified. Proceed to the machinery tests.
- Split outcome (moves at z=2, not at z=1.5) -> (A) and (C) separate
  cleanly by epoch, matching exp45's R80/R90 flip.

## Results (2026-08-12, full n=2397; `outputs/run_full.log`)

Pipeline validation: the untrimmed row reproduces the record exactly —
2.2 / 2.6 / 2.8 / 3.5 / 4.8 and 2.2 / 2.7 / 2.9 / 3.7 / 4.5 on the two
kpc planes, matching exp41 `outputs/qa_regen.log`.

**Headline: the z >= 1.5 plane failure is NOT a high-z phenomenon. It is
the model's failure on COMPACT galaxies, which is present at EVERY epoch
and dominates at high z only because high-z galaxies are compact.** None
of the three pre-registered outcomes covers this; the fourth is recorded
here as measured.

### 1a-1c — every "the target is not real" candidate died

| | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| stellar shot noise, M(30-50) at z=2 | **0.027 dex** median, 0.094 at the 95th pct, against **0.338** measured | 0.6% of the variance. Dead. |
| is the excess a low-mass tail? | at z=2 the BEST inner-mass quartile is still 0.288 dex (0.163 at z=0.4); Q1 0.455 | a bulk shift with an extra tail, not a tail alone |
| near-empty progenitors (exp37, >2 dex) | 0.1% at z=2 (4.8% beyond 1 dex) | negligible |

So the truth's growing high-z plane diversity is real structure: the
scatter on M(<30) vs M(30-50) rises 0.170 -> 0.338 dex, and on
M(<30) vs M(50-100) 0.204 -> 0.539, in every mass quartile.

### 1d — the trimmed re-score (p = fraction of 24 random same-n trims scoring at or below)

Only p is interpretable; the raw value falls for free when n shrinks.

| trim (tier 2b, M(<30) vs M(30-50)) | z=0.4 | z=0.7 | z=1.0 | z=1.5 | z=2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 2.2 | 2.6 | 2.8 | 3.5 | 4.8 |
| R50 >= 2 kpc (on grid) | 2.3 [1.00] | 2.7 [0.67] | 2.9 [0.08] | 3.4 [0.83] | 3.3 [0.21] |
| **R50 >= 5 kpc (resolved)** | **1.5 [0.00]** | **1.7 [0.00]** | **1.6 [0.00]** | **1.1 [0.00]** | **1.0 [0.00]** |
| top inner-mass quartile | 1.4 [0.96] | 1.1 [0.00] | 1.8 [0.75] | 2.3 [0.96] | 2.6 [1.00] |

1. **The resolved-size cut is significant at p = 0.00 at EVERY epoch**,
   including z = 0.4 where it drops only 6.3% of galaxies and still takes
   2.2 -> 1.5. That is what rules out a high-z-specific artifact: the
   same cut helps just as decisively at the anchor epoch, where sizes
   (median R50 11.6 kpc) are nowhere near a resolution limit. The reason
   it looks like a high-z problem is pure demographics — the fraction of
   the population with R50 < 5 kpc runs **6.3 / 14.0 / 27.4 / 60.7 /
   85.2%** across the five epochs.
2. **The failure is about SIZE, not MASS.** Restricting to the top
   inner-mass quartile does NOT help — at z >= 1.5 it is *worse* than a
   random subsample of the same size (p = 0.96 / 1.00 on plane 1,
   1.00 / 1.00 on plane 2). Consistent with the truth's mass-size slope
   collapsing to +0.08 by z = 2: size and mass are nearly independent
   there, and only the size axis carries the failure.
3. **The one genuine measurement artifact is real but confined to tier
   2d.** Dropping the 13.0% of z=2 galaxies whose R50 falls below the
   2.0 kpc CoG grid start (size obtained by log-log extrapolation) moves
   the mass-size plane 5.0 -> 3.5 (p = 0.04) while leaving tier 2b
   untouched (p = 0.21 / 0.17). Exactly as it should: tier 2b measures
   masses at 30-100 kpc, which do not care about R50. **So ~1.5 of tier
   2d's 5.0 at z=2 is an extrapolation artifact and should not be
   chased; none of tier 2b's 4.8 is.**
4. Metric hygiene, worth recording: removing a SINGLE galaxy (the 2-dex
   progenitor cut, n 2397 -> 2396) moved z=1.5 from 3.5 to 4.1, worse
   than all 24 random one-galaxy removals. `plane_energy` standardizes by
   the truth's per-axis std and estimates its floor from 8 half-splits,
   so single influential objects move it. This is why the whole table is
   reported as p rather than as a delta.

### Verdict

- **The tier-2b target is REAL.** No measurement artifact was found that
  explains any of it; the machinery campaign is justified on this metric.
- **But the problem is mis-named.** "The high-z ridge" is the model
  failing on compact galaxies. The efficiency-window hypothesis (high-z
  galaxies have no spread of arrival times to differ in) predicts a
  high-z-specific defect and therefore does **not**, on its own, explain
  a z=0.4 cut that is equally significant.
- **The leading unified hypothesis for stage 2**: compact galaxies are
  exactly the galaxies whose centres the model under-fills — exp45
  measured per-galaxy Spearman(dlog R50, M\*(<5 kpc) bias) = **-0.89 to
  -0.97 at every epoch**. If the compact-galaxy plane failure and the
  known inner-mass deficit are one defect, then exp40's dissipative-era
  finding is partially rehabilitated: not as a statement about *redshift*
  but as a statement about *compact galaxies*, which merely happen to be
  concentrated at high z.
